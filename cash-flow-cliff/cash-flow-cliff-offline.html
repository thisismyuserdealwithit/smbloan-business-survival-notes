The end of the month hides the middle.

A closing balance adds up the month. A daily forecast checks whether cash arrives before the payments it must cover. Both calculations can be right while telling different stories.

This exercise changes payment timing and starting cash. It does not calculate profit, tax, loan pricing, default probability or a lender's assessment.

What to try

  1. Guess the first negative day before opening the curve.
  2. Delay the selected customer's payment by seven days. Watch the lowest balance.
  3. Bring part of that payment forward as a deposit. Check what changes and what does not.
  4. Try the smallest extra opening buffer that keeps every day-end balance at or above zero.

A negative result is an unfunded cash gap in the model. The simulation continues to expose later dates; it does not assume a real bank would honour an overdrawn payment.

Methods and further reading

Daily cash = previous day's balance + receipts − outgoings. The required extra buffer is the amount needed to bring the lowest modelled balance to zero. Payments shifted beyond day 30 remain pending and are excluded from that month's closing balance.

The scenarios were created for this exercise. They are not estimates of typical Australian businesses. Amounts represent simplified cash movements with no separate GST calculation, interest, financing, tax assessment, insolvency test or holiday adjustment.
