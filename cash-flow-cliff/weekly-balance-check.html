Cash-flow Cliff

Would a weekly balance check catch payroll day?

A supplier starts with A$5,000. Materials cost A$6,000 on day 7, staff get A$4,000 on day 14 and overheads come to A$2,000 on day 21. There’s one customer invoice for A$15,000. Pick the day it gets paid and the day the bookkeeper starts checking.

Choose the two dates

Reporting calendar
With the receipt on day 15 and the first check on day 1, the weekly report shows a funding gap of A$1,000. The daily gap reaches A$5,000 on day 14. The report misses A$4,000.

The outlined days are on the bookkeeper’s report. “Short” marks a day the business cannot cover all scheduled payments. The bookkeeper also checks opening and closing cash.

    Try moving the receipt by one day

    Compare a receipt on day 14 with one on day 15. The month closes at A$8,000 in both cases. With a day-15 receipt, only one of the seven weekly schedules catches the full A$5,000 shortfall. Which schedule is it?

    Show the teacher’s answer

    Start the weekly checks on day 7. That puts both day 7 and day 14 on the report. The other six schedules still pick up an earlier A$1,000 gap, but miss the extra A$4,000 needed for payroll on day 14.
