Reading Australian business statistics

Business survival, with the starting count in view

A survival percentage describes a particular group of businesses. Compare five employment groups from the same ABS entry cohort, with the original counts beside each rate.

Entered in 2022–23 · Followed to June 2026 · Australia

Which group is the rate about?

Changing the group changes both counts. The cohort and June 2026 endpoint stay the same.

The denominator

The number of entrants in that group is the starting count. Divide the number that survived to June 2026 by that starting count, then multiply by 100.

132,708 ÷ 297,302 × 100 = 44.6%

“Non-employing” is an employment classification, not another name for sole trader. The rate is rounded to one decimal place.

A higher rate can describe fewer businesses

The 200+ employee group had 54 survivors from 69 entrants, giving 78.3%. The non-employing group had 132,708 survivors from 297,302 entrants, giving 44.6%. Keep those different starting counts visible when comparing the percentages.

Combine counts to find the overall rate

Across these five groups, 199,742 of 406,365 entrants survived to June 2026: 49.2%. Averaging the five group percentages would give each group equal weight, even though their sizes differ.

199,742 ÷ 406,365 × 100 = 49.2%

What these figures cannot tell you

  • ABS survival records continued activity within its business-register framework. It does not establish profit, adequate owner income or capacity to repay a loan.
  • An entry can include a reactivation. An exit can reflect closure, sale, restructuring or leaving the statistical scope. The complement of survival is not an insolvency rate.
  • These are historical cohort outcomes, not a current annual failure rate or the probability that a business starting today will survive.
  • Starting employment size does not establish that hiring causes survival. The cohort includes larger businesses; it is not restricted to small businesses or all registered ABN holders.
